Legal Analysis

Who committed an offence?

Claire BennettTreasurer
Claire physically completed the transfer, but the evidence shows she believed she was moving the money to protect it after receiving what she thought was a genuine bank security warning.
Legal conclusion for this case study: The evidence does not support theft by Claire because the case file does not establish dishonest intent or an intention to permanently deprive the community association of its money.
A serious mistake can cause loss without automatically becoming theft.
Alex MorganYouth member
Alex was present in the building and was seen near the hallway, but there is no evidence that Alex accessed the bank account or made the transfer.
Legal conclusion: The evidence does not establish that Alex committed an offence.
Presence, rumours and suspicion are not proof.
Daniel ReedCouncillor
Councillor Reed opposed the project and was present at the community centre, but the evidence does not connect him to the bank transfer or fraudulent email.
Legal conclusion: Political disagreement may suggest motive in a mystery, but motive alone is not evidence of criminal conduct.
Unknown Scam OperatorUnidentified
The fraudulent email was designed to deceive the account holder into transferring funds to an account controlled by another person or group.
Legal conclusion for the investigation: The strongest evidence points to an unknown offender using deception to obtain control of the community funds.
The identity of that offender is not established by the student case file. Investigators must not invent an identity.
Classroom Court

Suggested Court Proceedings

1. Court Opens

Magistrate/Judge: “This court will consider the evidence presented in relation to the disappearance of funds from the Banksia Creek Community Association. The court reminds all participants that allegations must be tested against evidence and law.”

2. Student Legal Teams Present

Each student or team presents their lawyer’s opening statement and final case theory. The class listens for:

  • accurate use of evidence
  • correct chronology
  • legal principles
  • acknowledgement of uncertainty
  • fair treatment of each person involved

3. Evidence Challenge

The teacher may ask one or two questions after each presentation:

  • Which exhibit is strongest?
  • What evidence weakens your argument?
  • What would the prosecution still need to prove?
  • Have you treated any claim as if it were a fact?
  • Would your conclusion meet the burden of proof?

4. Final Evidence Released

Reveal the confirmed banking and device information above. Students compare it with their original theory.

5. Court Finding

Suggested finding: “The evidence does not establish that Alex Morgan, Daniel Reed or Claire Bennett committed theft. The evidence indicates that Claire Bennett was deceived into transferring the funds. The suspected criminal conduct lies with the unidentified person or group responsible for the fraudulent email and receiving account.”

Case Outcome

Alex Morgan

No offence established by the case evidence.

Claire Bennett

Made the transfer, but dishonest criminal intent is not established.

Unknown scam operator

Strongest evidence of deceptive criminal conduct; identity remains unknown.

Final Civics & Citizenship Reflection

Students answer these after the final reveal:

  1. Which piece of evidence caused you to change or strengthen your theory?
  2. Why was the presumption of innocence important for Alex?
  3. Why is the burden of proof important when evidence is incomplete?
  4. How did the independent court process produce a fairer outcome than rumours or media speculation?
  5. What does this case show about the rule of law?
  6. If citizens believed the law or legal process needed to change, how could they participate in that change?
