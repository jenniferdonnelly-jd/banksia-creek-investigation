FINAL TASK: You have completed the investigation. Your job now is to communicate your case as a legal team. Your conclusion must be based on evidence — not on who you personally think is suspicious.
Part A

Your Lawyer's Opening Statement

An opening statement explains the case your legal team believes the evidence will support. It should be clear, factual and persuasive, without exaggerating what the evidence can prove.
Suggested structure:

“Members of the court,

The evidence in this case will show that …

On Friday, 4 September 2026 …

You will hear evidence from …

Exhibit ___ demonstrates …

The relevant law requires …

Our case is that …

However, we also acknowledge that …

At the conclusion of this case, we will ask you to find that …”
Evidence requirement: Your opening statement must refer to at least three documentary exhibits and two witness statements.
Higher-level response: Include one piece of evidence that weakens your argument and explain why you do not think it changes your overall conclusion.
Part B

Choose Your Final Product

Option 1 — Lawyer's Case Presentation PowerPoint
Create a short PowerPoint that presents your case to the class as if you were a legal team briefing the court.
SlideWhat to include
1The Case: case title, issue and your main conclusion.
2Timeline: the key events in chronological order.
3Key Evidence: your strongest exhibits and witness statements.
4The Law: the legal principles and offence you applied.
5Our Case: your final argument and what remains uncertain.
Presentation length: approximately 3–5 minutes.
Option 2 — Investigative News Article News Report
Write a front-page investigative article that explains the case accurately and fairly.

Your article must include:

  • a clear headline
  • a factual summary of the case
  • a short timeline
  • evidence from at least three exhibits
  • information from at least two witnesses
  • the relevant law
  • what is proven and what remains uncertain
  • a careful final conclusion
Media challenge: Avoid writing that a person is “guilty” unless the evidence and legal process actually establish that. Think about presumption of innocence.
Part C

What Your Final Case Must Demonstrate

Final Case Checklist

  • accurate timeline
  • clear case theory
  • at least three documentary exhibits
  • at least two witness statements
  • fact distinguished from inference
  • contradictory evidence considered
  • Victorian law applied correctly
  • rule of law explained
  • presumption of innocence considered
  • burden of proof considered
  • what remains uncertain identified
  • clear and persuasive communication

What You Are Being Assessed On

Assessment area What strong work shows
Investigating & analysing Uses the timeline, documents and witness statements accurately and identifies important contradictions.
Evaluating evidence Explains the strength and limitations of evidence rather than simply listing it.
Laws & citizens Applies relevant Victorian law and principles such as rule of law, presumption of innocence and burden of proof.
Democratic institutions Explains why courts, due process and an independent legal system matter.
Communicating Presents a clear, logical and evidence-based case suited to the chosen format.

Before You Submit

1. Evidence Check

Can every major claim in your final case be traced back to an exhibit or witness statement?

2. Fairness Check

Have you avoided treating suspicion, rumours or opinions as proof?

3. Communication Check

Could someone who has never seen the case file understand your argument?

Do not open any final reveal until instructed by your teacher. Your assessment is based on how well you investigate, evaluate evidence, apply the law and communicate your case — not on whether your first guess matches the final explanation.
