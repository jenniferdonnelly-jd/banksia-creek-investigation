Part D

Write Your Case Theory

Your case theory should answer:

WHAT happened to the $18,750?
WHEN did the key events occur?
WHO was involved in the important actions?
WHY do you believe this explanation best fits the evidence?
WHAT EVIDENCE supports your conclusion?
WHAT EVIDENCE weakens or limits your conclusion?
WHAT LAW applies?
WHAT CANNOT YET BE PROVEN?
Suggested opening:
“After examining the documentary evidence, witness statements and relevant Victorian law, our case is that…”
Higher-level response: It is acceptable to conclude that the evidence does not prove that a particular person committed an offence. A legal conclusion must follow the evidence, not the desire to identify a culprit.

Case File Quality Check

Before moving to your final presentation, make sure your case:

  • uses the timeline accurately
  • refers to exhibits by name or letter
  • uses at least two witness statements
  • distinguishes fact from inference
  • explains contradictory evidence
  • applies relevant legal principles
  • considers presumption of innocence
  • identifies what remains uncertain
  • does not invent missing evidence
  • reaches a clear, evidence-based conclusion

Your Case Preparation

Complete these three products in your Humanities book or investigation booklet.

1. Case Theory

Write one clear paragraph explaining your version of events.

2. Evidence List

Select your five strongest pieces of evidence and explain what each one proves.

3. Legal Position

State whether you believe an offence can currently be proven, and justify your answer.
